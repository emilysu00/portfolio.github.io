Homie
A Support System for Young Renters

Overview

Young renters often face legal uncertainty due to fragmented information and unclear procedures.Homie explores how design can intervene across the rental journey to address moments of anxiety and uncertainty.Through service and app design, the project aims to reduce rental risks for young renters.

Year

2025.March - 2025.June

Role

UX Researcher, System Designer
Team project with TzuHsuan Chen, Ying Ci Su, and Jing Ya Chen

Background & Research

 Design Solution
Background Research

According to background research, approximately 43.9% of people in Taiwan have experienced rental disputes, with around 4,000 housing rental dispute cases occurring each year.Due to insufficient dormitory supply at universities, more than 50% of students are required to rent housing off campus. At the same time, people aged 20–29 account for 29.4% of the total rental population, indicating that young adults have become a primary group within the rental market.
Through a survey (n = 85), we identified several recurring challenges commonly faced by young renters.

44%

struggle to evaluate housing conditions during viewings

32%

overlook critical clauses when signing contracts

20%

experience landlord disputes without clear support channels發生與房東的糾紛時，缺乏求助管道

Personas & User Journey Mapping

Through user interviews (n = 5), personas were developed by synthesizing and clustering interview insights. Based on these findings, user journey maps and target personas were created, identifying key intervention points for design based on renters’ contexts and challenges.

Design Solution
Design Overview

Homie is designed as a human-in-the-loop support system that helps renters navigate the rental process, from property viewings to contracts, and connects them with a supportive legal community when issues arise.

Service Blueprint

Homie frames the rental process as a coordinated system supported by interfaces, data, and human support. The service blueprint aligns user needs with technical feasibility and ethical considerations.
