Overview

Street tree issues significantly affect everyday urban life, yet existing maintenance systems often respond slowly and lack transparency.This project explores how interactive mechanisms can encourage public engagement, enhance decision-making efficiency, and, through a scan–report–track system, restructure how citizen observations are translated into urban tree maintenance actions.

Background & Design Framework

Through a survey (n = 40), we found that residents are generally aware of street tree issues and that most have been affected by them. However, this awareness rarely translates into action.Based on this insight, we framed the design question around how to turn awareness into action, and developed an easy-to-use and engaging reporting system aimed at transforming public concern into tangible actions that improve everyday urban life.

Awareness

Connect awareness through everyday encounters with urban issues.

Participation

Access participation through an intuitive reporting system.

Continuity

Maintain engagement through interaction by building a traceable system that encourages continued participation.

Interviews

We interviewed students and faculty members involved in urban planning and identified several structural challenges.Our findings showed that while the initial design improved the reporting process, it failed to enhance institutional response at a systemic level. Without addressing this gap, the system risked becoming yet another unused reporting channel.

1. Lack of clear regulations

Street trees in Taiwan are managed by multiple government offices, often without clearly defined regulations.

2. Lack of professional expertise

Tree maintenance is often outsourced to contractors, making outcomes heavily dependent on their judgment.

What if we design a clearer and more efficient workflow for government agencies?
