Treasure Hill Artist Village
Rebranding

Honorable Mention, Taipei Design Award

Overview

Focusing on Treasure Hill Artist Village as the research subject, this project combines in-depth research, on-site exploration, and brand redesign to better integrate cultural context and visual identity into the site, making branding an active part of the spatial experience.

Motivation
Cultural Empowerment

Could visual integration and spatial redesign make Treasure Hill itself a landmark exhibition?

Tourism Development

Taiwan has been promoting tourism projects, with cultural tourism becoming an immersive experience for tourists to integrate with local residents (Richards, 2000).

Branding

Design focuses on its distinctive cultural and artistic atmosphere, to attract more investment and collaboration opportunities.

Survey & Field Research Analysis

On-site visits were conducted at Treasure Hill, along with interviews and surveys with 54 visitors.Through field observation, key strengths and challenges of the site were identified.

Strengths
  • Located in Taipei, yet offering a unique environment that allows visitors to step away from fast-paced urban life.
  • The artist-in-residence model supports long-term operation, with flexible outdoor spaces and regularly updated exhibition content.
    • Challenges
  • Unclear way-finding makes it difficult to locate entrances, artworks, and current position.
  • Visitors often lack understanding of the site’s cultural, environmental, and community-based context.
  • Low visibility of exhibitions and events leads to shallow, sightseeing-focused visits.
  • Inconsistent visual identity due to the absence of a unified design system.

    Initial Brand Positioning

    Color (in / out) Side reflects the multifaceted nature of Treasure Hill Artist Village.The open, pixel-like brackets symbolize the site’s ambiguous boundaries, allowing visitors to freely define what is considered “inside” and “outside.” Combined with a flexible, multi-style typographic system, the brand expresses creativity and the village’s open, unconventional way of thinking.

    Color Plan

    Architectural gray, roof-tile red, leaf green, and sky blue were selected as the primary colors and translated into a cohesive visual application system.

    Supporting Illustrations

    Supporting illustrations are based on four representative objects from the Treasure Hill settlement, along with the curved pathways as visual support.

    Design Update

    Environmental design shifts the focus back to the space itself.

    Brand Repositioning

    The core spirit of Treasure Hill is distilled into the idea of “Self-Built Creation.”Based on the perspectives of residents, artists, and visitors, three supporting concepts were defined: Building, Creating, and Visiting. Together, these ideas form a community where art and living coexist, allowing visitors to become part of the site’s cultural ecosystem.
